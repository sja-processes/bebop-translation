# Be-Bop High School — English guide to the original manual

An English guide to the Japanese instruction booklet (Data East, 1988,
DFC-EP, 28 pages plus covers), written for this translation project.
It covers everything the manual explains, in the manual's own order, but
summarises the story banter and character blurbs rather than translating
them line by line. Command and stat names are given as they appear in our
English version, with the Japanese in brackets.

The manual is written as a school timetable of five "lessons", each
presented by one of the characters in their own voice.

---

## Lesson 1 — The High-School Paradise Legend (story set-up, pp. 2-7)

Told as a manga-style exchange between Toru and Hiroshi. Hiroshi has
noticed that Shingo of Kita High seemed unusually serious the day before;
Toru shrugs it off and teases Hiroshi about Remi of Chuo Girls' High,
whom Shingo promised to introduce. Then Shingo's younger hanger-on
rushes up: Shingo was snatched by someone that morning, and had said
that if anything happened to him, Kato and Nakama of Aitoku should be
told first.

The rival duo stake their claim to Remi and mock the heroes for not even
knowing who took Shingo; the heroes and the rivals agree to a race —
whoever rescues Shingo first gets Remi. Nobuo announces he is coming too,
and the pair skip lessons to start the hunt. (This is the same scene as
the game's opening, message `238`.)

## Lesson 2 — Character profiles (pp. 8-13)

Presented by Toru and Hiroshi "showing their profiles". In brief:

- **Hiroshi Kato** — Aitoku High, 2nd year, class F. Quick to fight and
  always in trouble with other schools, so the teachers have him marked
  as a problem pupil; but he has a strong sense of loyalty and decency,
  helps the weak and stands up to bullies.
- **Toru Nakama** — Hiroshi's classmate. A master fighter, famous for
  his slicked-back quiff. Best mate and rival rolled into one; nobody
  quite knows what he's thinking. Like Hiroshi, hopeless with girls.
- **Shinpei Kuroda** — Aitoku, 1st year. Sly by nature, kind to girls
  but never gets anywhere with them. On this adventure he tags along
  with **Toru** (and Nobuo).
- **Kintaro Omae** — Shinpei's friend since middle school. Call him
  "Pig" and he goes berserk and can't be stopped. He tags along with
  **Hiroshi**.
- **Nobuo Kaneko** — Aitoku, 2nd year. The self-appointed number one
  sidekick of both heroes. Can't abide anything underhand; slightly dim,
  and fond of high places (a play on the saying that fools and smoke
  both rise to the top).
- **Junko Miharayama** — Aitoku, 3rd year; was in the heroes' year when
  they were in 2nd year. A beauty with a reputation for being cold to
  men; a strong-willed older girl.
- **Misako** — a cute 16-year-old schoolgirl. The manual's point: when
  your **luck is good**, townspeople will give you all sorts of
  information, the way she does.
- **Shingo Maekawa** — Kita High, 3rd year. A ferociously strong fighter
  who loves women even more. This time he chatted up someone else's
  girlfriend and got himself kidnapped.
- **Toshimitsu Yamada** — Joto High, 3rd year. Champion-class fighter;
  not a noisy small-timer but a genuine hard man who sticks to his
  principles.
- **Tomezo** — an old man who's always at the pachinko parlour. He has
  lost his false teeth, so he's hard to understand, but he's generous.
  (Our English renders this as his lisp.)
- **Onijima** — "Demon Island" for short. You'd never guess from his
  looks or talk, but he's a police sergeant in the juvenile division,
  and the heroes can't stand up to him.

## Lesson 3 — Operation Rescue the Delinquent (how to play, pp. 14-19)

Presented by Nobuo. The game: gather information from all sorts of
people in town and in shops, and rescue Shingo of Kita High.

### Starting a game
- On the title screen choose a hero with **Select**, confirm with
  **Start**. Hiroshi is accompanied by Kintaro, Toru by Shinpei; Nobuo
  goes with either. Press **A** to listen to the opening story.
- To resume, choose **Continue** (つづきをヤル). The cartridge has a
  battery backup and resumes from where you last saved. A new game
  starts at Aitoku High.

### Stopping and saving
- To stop, go back to **your hero's home** and choose **Save** (セーブする)
  (D-pad to select, **A** to confirm). Nobuo "remembers" all the
  information gathered so far.
- When switching the console off, **hold Reset** while you do so, to
  protect the save.

### The two screens
- **Map screen** — walk round town with the D-pad. Meeting someone or
  entering a building switches to the background screen.
- **Background screen** — a picture of the scene with the **command
  menu** above it. Choose a command (D-pad, then **A**); the result
  appears as a message. The **NERVE** gauge and **LUCK** grade are shown
  top left.

### LUCK (うんせい, 運勢)
Luck colours everything; with bad luck even the heroes have off days.
**Every morning, draw a fortune slip (omikuji) at the shrine** to see
your luck for the day. There are five grades, best to worst:

| Ours | Japanese | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| CHARM | ばり吉 (bari-kichi) | slang "mega good luck" |
| GOOD | 大吉 (dai-kichi) | great luck |
| FAIR | 中吉 (chū-kichi) | middling luck |
| BAD | 凶 (kyō) | bad luck |
| JINX | どつぼ (dotsubo) | rock bottom |

### NERVE (きりょく, 気力)
Full NERVE means the heroes are at full power. It **drops when you lose
a fight or get turned down by a girl**. When evening comes, **go home
early and sleep**; staying out late also drains it. If it runs out
completely, it's **GAME OVER** and you won't get up again.

## Lesson 4 — The commands explained (pp. 20-23)

Presented by Toru and Hiroshi. The commands shown on the background
screen when you meet someone or go into a building:

| Ours | Japanese | What the manual says |
|---|---|---|
| Leave | いどうする (移動する, "move") | Move away from the person you've met, or from where you are inside a building. |
| Talk | はなす (話す) | Talk with the person. No need to start a fight straight away — keep it peaceful. |
| Ask | きく (聞く) | When you want to hear more detail about what they said. |
| Lean on | おどす (脅す) | For someone who won't tell you anything when you talk or ask nicely: intimidate them. |
| Thump | なぐる (殴る) | For someone words won't get through to — one punch. But look carefully at who you're choosing. |
| Sleep | ねる (寝る) | A clean, upright schoolboy goes to bed early at home. |
| Search | しらべる (調べる) | Check for suspicious people or anything out of place. |
| Buy | かう (買う) | For buying things such as fortune slips. Steal them and you'll pay for it (bad luck). |
| Go up | のぼる (登る) | Move up inside a building. (Joke: "It's not someone's name, so calling it won't help" — Noboru is a name.) |
| Go down | おりる (降りる) | The opposite of Go up. (Same joke.) |
| Save | セーブする | Only at Toru's or Hiroshi's home (see Lesson 3). Nobuo remembers everything learned so far. |

The manual adds that **there are more commands than these**, which it
deliberately doesn't explain: try them and find out (even Hiroshi
managed). Ours: Take, Flatter, Coffee, Use, Promise, Tease, Watch, Beg,
Give, Shout, Ride, Sneak, Chase.

## Lesson 5 — Basic skills course (tips, pp. 24-28)

Four tips, presented by an older lad in a loud shirt:

1. **Flatter the cheeky ones if you have to.** At the start nobody knows
   who took Shingo or where, so the job is to walk round town gathering
   information: go into buildings and search, and go for a coffee
   (Coffee, おちゃする) with girls you meet and listen to what they say.
   Sometimes you'll have to suck up to people you can't stand. Grin and
   bear it — it's for Shingo (supposedly).
2. **Luck counts as skill.** Everything the heroes do is affected by
   NERVE and by that day's LUCK. With bad luck you'll be turned down by
   girls and lose fights, NERVE will fall, you won't be able to gather
   information, and you may end up at GAME OVER. Watch it.
3. **No staying out at night.** Head home in the evening. Roaming round
   after dark tires you out and drains NERVE, and if you're unlucky the
   police will pick you up. Night-time fun is no joke.
4. **For those who won't listen to words...** The commands on offer and
   the replies you get change with who you meet and the situation. Some
   people don't understand talking — and might need a single punch.

### Notes for the translation

- Our stat and command names match the manual's concepts: NERVE =
  きりょく, LUCK = うんせい, Leave = いどうする, Lean on = おどす,
  Flatter = ごまする ("suck up"), Coffee = おちゃする.
- The manual confirms the luck order we used (ばり吉 best, どつぼ worst).
- Daily loop implied by the manual: shrine fortune in the morning →
  gather information round town → home to sleep in the evening; save at
  home.
- Partner names: Hiroshi + **Kintaro**, Toru + **Shinpei** (glossary
  doesn't list them yet).
